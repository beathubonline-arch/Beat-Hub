FIELD GUIDE · VOLUME 01

A Beginner's Guide to Life Before Civilization

A short starting point for curious viewers, not a substitute for scientific scholarship.

1. What counts as prehistory?

Prehistory refers to periods before written records in a given society. Its boundaries vary by place. Archaeologists examine tools, bones, sediments, sites and other physical evidence to investigate lives not described in surviving texts.

2. A very brief timeline

  • Millions of years ago: early hominins lived in Africa; multiple human lineages later emerged.
  • Roughly 300,000 years ago: early Homo sapiens existed in Africa, based on current fossil evidence.
  • Tens of thousands of years ago: people had diverse symbolic and technological traditions in many regions.
  • About 12,000 years ago onward: farming developed independently in several places, at different times.

3. What happened after dark?

Fire sometimes provided warmth, light, a place for food preparation and social gathering. But practices varied hugely by environment, period and community. Archaeological traces do not tell us exactly what every ancient evening looked like.

4. What did people eat?

There was no single prehistoric diet. People hunted, scavenged, gathered plant foods, fished and used whatever foods their environments and technologies made available. Teeth, bones, tools and food remains can provide clues.

5. Tools and shelter

Stone tools survive particularly well, but prehistoric technologies could also involve wood, fiber, skin and other perishable materials. Shelters likewise varied, from caves and rock overhangs to structures that rarely survive clearly.

6. How to separate evidence from imagination

Ask three questions: What remains have actually been found? What do researchers infer from them? What parts of a reconstruction are speculative?

Quick quiz

  1. Did farming begin everywhere at exactly the same time?
  2. Does the absence of written documents mean we know nothing about a community?
  3. Can a cinematic reenactment show exactly what happened one prehistoric night?

Answers: No; no; no.
